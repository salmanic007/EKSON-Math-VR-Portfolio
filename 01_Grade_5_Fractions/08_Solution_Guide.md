        <h2>1. Step-by-Step Solution for Core Problem</h2>
        <div class="warning">
            <strong>Problem:</strong> Add 2/8 + 3/8
        </div>
        
        <div class="step-box">
            <span class="step-number">1</span>
            <strong>Identify the denominators.</strong> Both are 8. (They are "like" denominators).
        </div>
        
        <div class="step-box">
            <span class="step-number">2</span>
            <strong>Keep the denominator the same</strong> (8).
        </div>
        
        <div class="step-box">
            <span class="step-number">3</span>
            <strong>Add the numerators:</strong> 2 + 3 = 5.
        </div>
        
        <div class="step-box">
            <span class="step-number">4</span>
            <strong>Write the final fraction:</strong> 5/8.
        </div>
        
        <div class="step-box">
            <span class="step-number">5</span>
            <strong>Verify visually:</strong> Count the total pieces on the virtual plate (5 pieces) out of the original whole (8 pieces).
        </div>

        <h2>2. Common Misconceptions & How to Address Them in VR</h2>
        <table class="misconception-table">
            <tr>
                <th>Misconception</th>
                <th>Why students make it</th>
                <th>VR Feedback / Correction Strategy</th>
            </tr>
            <tr>
                <td><strong>Adding denominators:</strong><br>2/8 + 3/8 = 5/16</td>
                <td>Student applies a rote rule of "adding top and bottom" without understanding the concept of the "whole".</td>
                <td><strong>Visual Proof:</strong> Show the student that the chocolate bar still only has 8 pieces total, not 16. The denominator represents the whole, which didn't change.</td>
            </tr>
            <tr>
                <td><strong>Comparing denominators:</strong><br>Thinking 2/8 is bigger than 5/8 because 8 is big</td>
                <td>Confusing the size of the "whole" with the size of the "parts".</td>
                <td><strong>Visual Proof:</strong> Place the 2-piece stack and the 5-piece stack side-by-side in VR. Ask: "Which pile is physically taller?"</td>
            </tr>
            <tr>
                <td><strong>Ignoring the "Whole":</strong></td>
                <td>Student forgets what the denominator refers to.</td>
                <td><strong>Narrator Prompt:</strong> "Remember, the bottom number is the total pieces the entire bar was cut into."</td>
            </tr>
